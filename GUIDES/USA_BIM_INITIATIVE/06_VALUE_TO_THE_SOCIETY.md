Not to individual architects — to the **institution**. A professional body says yes to
things that help it discharge its own mandate. This maps the platform onto that mandate.

Ordered by strength of fit. The first three are the ones to build the presentation on.

## 1 · Statutory CPD — the strongest fit by a distance

**Every practising architect in Uganda needs 20 CPD points a year to renew their
practising licence**, under the Architects Registration (Continuing Professional
Development) Bye Laws, gazetted 2019. This is not optional and it does not go away.

So the Society has a permanent, recurring obligation to put credible content in front
of its members — and BIM and ISO 19650 is exactly the content that is hardest to source
locally. Most CPD available to Ugandan architects is product-vendor presentations.

What the platform offers here:

- A structured, hands-on programme rather than a lecture: ISO 19650 data standards, a
  working walkthrough of a model being tagged and audited, coordination workflow,
  handover data — built around an exercise members actually do
- Repeatable in cohorts, so it is a programme rather than an event
- Delivered by a member, so the Society is not importing a trainer

What the Society gets: a flagship CPD programme it owns and brands, an educational
mandate discharged, and content its members must buy anyway.

**This is also a funding route, not only a benefit** — see `07_FUNDING_ROUTES.md`.
Members pay for CPD because the law requires it. Cohort fees can make delivery
self-sustaining without anyone borrowing anything.

**Ask the Society, don't assume:** what ARB accreditation a programme needs, how points
are awarded for a multi-day course, and what members currently pay for CPD. They know;
you don't. Asking is also a good way to make them co-owners of the idea.

## 2 · Members getting locked out of their own market — the "why now"

**MoWT is deliberately excluded from this argument** (Davis, 2026-09-08 — that
conversation is still pending and is not to be used here). The case stands without it,
and is arguably cleaner for a professional body, because it positions the Society as
leading rather than reacting.

This is the argument that should open the substantive part of the presentation, because
it makes the problem **theirs**, not yours.

- **ISO 19650 is now the accepted international standard** for managing project
  information — naming, versioning, approval, handover.
- **Governments have started mandating BIM on public work** — the United Kingdom, the
  UAE, Singapore and Germany among them. It has only ever moved in one direction.
- **Kenya, the obvious regional comparison, is documented as lagging**, with poor
  coordination of information between project parties named as the consequence.
- **Uganda has neither a national information standard for architectural delivery nor
  any locally built, locally supported tooling.**

So a practice with no answer to "how will information be delivered" stops appearing on
shortlists and never learns why; it competes against international and regional firms
that already work this way; and it pays for the shortfall on site, where coordination
errors are most expensive and the architect usually carries the blame.

A professional body exists to protect its members' standing and livelihood. **A change
in what clients expect, that its members are not ready for, is precisely its business.**

**Say "the direction of travel", never "it is being mandated here".** There is no
Ugandan mandate. Claiming one is the fastest way to lose the room, and someone will
check.

## 3 · A national standard the Society authors — Board of Practice

The Board of Practice owns practice standards and documentation quality. Publishing a
standard is easy; getting anyone to comply is not. Standards die because compliance is
expensive and unverifiable.

What is different here: the platform ships a **working implementation**, not a document.
Naming and tagging schemes, drawing types, title blocks, view templates — and an audit
that *checks* a model against them and reports what fails. A standard that software can
enforce is a standard that gets used.

And if a national standards conversation ever convenes in Uganda, **the Society
arrives with a working draft rather than an opinion** — which is the difference
between chairing that conversation and being consulted on it.

Value to the Society: authority on a question nobody else here has answered, and a
visible contribution to the profession it did not have to manufacture.

## 4 · Small-practice economics — a member benefit the Society can negotiate

Most members are small practices. Per-seat licensing at Ugandan fee levels is punishing.

- Priced **per organisation**, not per person
- **Unlimited free external members** — so a three-person practice can bring the client,
  contractor and QS onto a project at no licence cost to anyone
- Billed in shillings
- Offline-capable for sites without connectivity

A Society-negotiated member rate is the most conventional professional-body benefit
there is, and it costs the Society nothing to ask for. Offer it before they do.

---

## 5 · Documentation quality, disputes and professional risk — Board of Practice

Architects are routinely blamed for coordination failures that were not theirs, and
conduct complaints often come down to what can be evidenced.

- Automated model and drawing audit catches inconsistency before issue
- Consistent naming and revision control means the record of what was issued, when, and
  to whom, exists without anyone maintaining it by hand
- A clash report is evidence that a conflict was raised and when

This is the Board of Practice's language: competence, evidence, and fewer disputes. It
also supports fee defence — demonstrable deliverables justify fees in a market where
architects are constantly asked to discount.

## 6 · The graduate pipeline — Board of Education

Makerere, Kyambogo and the other schools produce graduates who arrive without the tools
practices need. The Council has Graduate, Student and Technician representatives, so
these are represented constituencies, not an abstraction.

Student and graduate access to the platform means graduates arrive employable and
practices carry less training cost. **Be transparent that this is mutual** — it also
builds the platform's user base. Saying so costs nothing and pre-empts the observation.

## 7 · A local product, locally hosted — the ICT Cluster's agenda

Aimed squarely at the ICT Cluster chair:

- Built in Kampala, for Ugandan conditions
- Can be hosted in Uganda; data need not leave the country
- Open, exportable formats throughout — IFC 4, BCF 2.1, COBie, RVT, DWG, Excel, PDF —
  so nothing is trapped
- A priced exit path to the all-Autodesk stack, so adoption is reversible
- Subscription spend stays in the domestic economy instead of being exported

The Society backing a Ugandan-built product is a position the ICT Cluster can defend
publicly. That matters to them more than any feature.

## 8 · Uganda-specific content no imported tool carries

Regional defaults by Ugandan region — wind speed, seismic zone, soil bearing capacity,
design rainfall intensity, live loads — plus local code defaults, shilling budgets, and
offline-first working for sites with no connectivity.

This is the Society's own operating context encoded in software. It is also the clearest
answer to "why not just use what everyone else uses".

## 9 · The Society's own operations — modest, but say it

Document control for the secretariat, committee papers, awards and competition
submissions, and a record of what was circulated and when.

Small, but "we use it ourselves" is a credibility asset when the Society advocates it to
anyone else. **Do not oversell this one** — it is a footnote, not an argument.

## The limits — say these yourself, first

An architects' body will find these in about four minutes if you don't.

| Limit | How to put it |
|---|---|
| **StingTools is a Revit add-in, and many Ugandan architects use ArchiCAD** | The single most likely objection in this specific room. PlanScape is authoring-agnostic through IFC, and there is an ArchiCAD bridge, but it is early. Say so plainly and say what the roadmap is. If this lands as a gotcha rather than a disclosure, it costs you the ICT chair. |
| MEP calculation engines are not independently validated | Complete and tested, never carried through independent professional validation. Offered only as separately commissioned services, with manual cross-checks in parallel. Then invite the Society's engineers to be the validators — it turns a weakness into a role for them. |
| Production-ready for one deployment, not productised for many | The wording in `05_CONSISTENT_NARRATIVE.md`. Volunteer it. |
| There is no Ugandan BIM mandate | Say "the direction of travel". Never imply one exists. |

## What to actually build the 20 minutes around

Three arguments, in this order. Everything else is supporting material.

1. **A change is coming to your members' market** (§2) — makes the problem theirs
2. **Here is what exists, watch it work** (the demo) — makes it real
3. **Here is what the Society gets** (§1 CPD, §3 the standard, §4 member economics) —
   makes it worth their while

Then, and only then, what is left to finish and what it costs — and the ask, which is
help finding the route, not a cheque.
