# Funding routes the Society can open

**The ask changed on 2026-09-08.** Not "lend me your balance sheet" — *"here is what
this does for your members; help me find the way to fund it."*

That is a much easier thing for a professional body to say yes to. But it only works if
you arrive with options. "Help me find funding" invites a sympathetic nod and nothing
else. **"Here are six routes, four of which only the Society can open — which do you
think is worth pursuing?"** invites a decision.

Print this. Take it in. It is the page that turns goodwill into an action.

---

## What is actually being funded

Keep this honest and unchanged from the costed work already done:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Platform completion — multi-organisation support, payments, security review, testing | USD 36,200 |
| Hosting sized for regional use, 12 months | USD 12,000 |
| Training programme, three cohorts of ~25 | USD 15,000 |
| **Subtotal** | **USD 63,200** |
| Contingency 15% | USD 9,480 |
| **Total** | **USD 72,680** (~UGX 269M) |

**These are separable, and saying so makes the ask far easier.** Nobody has to find
USD 72,680. The training programme can be funded on its own and start first. Say that
explicitly — a funder who can only reach USD 15,000 is still a funder.

---

## The routes

Ordered by how realistic they are, not how much they raise.

### 1 · CPD cohort fees — the members fund it themselves

**Why it works:** CPD is statutory. Every practising architect needs 20 points a year to
renew a licence, so members already budget for training. This is not asking them to fund
a platform; it is selling them something the law requires them to buy.

**What the Society unlocks:** accreditation, its member list, and its name on the
programme. None of which costs it money.

**What you supply:** the programme, the delivery, the materials.

**Realistic scale:** likely covers the cost of delivering the training, and possibly a
margin the Society shares. **Unlikely on its own to fund platform completion** — say so
rather than implying otherwise.

**What you don't know and must ask:** what members currently pay for CPD in Kampala, and
what ARB accreditation requires. The Society knows both. Asking makes them co-owners.

---

### 2 · Development partners and donors — the biggest prize, and only they can open it

**Why it works:** World Bank, AfDB, GIZ, FCDO, EU, JICA and UN-Habitat all fund
construction-sector capacity building and digital skills. A **professional body is a
fundable grantee**; a young private company generally is not. This is the single
clearest thing the Society has that you do not.

**The pitch that fits their windows:** local digital capacity in the construction
sector, delivered by the national professional body, using locally built tooling, with a
measurable output (X architects trained, a national information standard drafted).

**What you supply:** the technical content, the platform, and the training delivery.

**What to ask for in the room:** does the Society have existing relationships with any
development partner, and who holds them? That is a name and a phone number, and it is
worth more than a resolution.

**Be realistic on timing:** donor cycles run months, not weeks. Worth starting now
precisely because it is slow.

---

### 3 · Industry sponsorship of the CPD programme

**Why it works:** cement, steel, roofing and glazing manufacturers, banks, insurers and
telcos routinely sponsor professional CPD for access and visibility with the profession.
A named cohort — the sort of thing a materials company likes its logo on — is an easy
sell for a body with the Society's standing.

**What the Society unlocks:** its convening power and its existing corporate
relationships. Sponsors pay for access to members; only the Society can grant that.

**What you supply:** a programme worth sponsoring.

**Realistic scale:** enough to fund cohorts. Possibly more if a sponsor wants a
multi-year association.

---

### 4 · Government and the MoWT route

**Why it works:** if the Ministry proceeds with a BIM mandate, capacity building stops
being a nice idea and becomes a public need — and the Society is the obvious delivery
partner for the architectural profession. The MoWT proposal §13 already recommends
engaging the universities and the professional bodies.

**What the Society unlocks:** standing to be that partner, which an individual does not
have.

**Caution:** this is contingent on a mandate that has not been issued. Present it as an
opportunity to prepare for, never as funding that exists.

---

### 5 · Uganda innovation and ICT funding

**Why it works:** a locally built software product with regional export potential fits
national digital-economy priorities. Candidates worth checking: NITA-U, the Ministry of
ICT and National Guidance innovation funding, UNCST, and the Uganda Industrial Research
Institute.

**What the Society unlocks:** institutional backing, which materially strengthens an
application from a small developer.

**What to check:** whether any of these fund software product completion as opposed to
research. Genuinely unknown — do not assume.

---

### 6 · University research partnership — Board of Education and Board of R&D

**Why it works:** a co-application with Makerere or Kyambogo reaches research funding
neither party reaches alone, and curriculum integration is a natural deliverable. Arch.
Daniel Sekamwa (Education) and Arch. Clare Ruhweza (Research and Development) are the
Council members whose remits this sits in — neither is currently in the meeting.

**What you supply:** the platform, and student access.

---

### 7 · Prepaid member subscriptions — a pre-sale, not a loan

**Why it works:** practices commit to a discounted multi-year subscription, paid up
front. That is revenue, not debt: no interest, no guarantee, no lender.

**What the Society unlocks:** the aggregation. Individually these are small; assembled
through the Society they are a funding round.

**The honest risk, and say it:** taking money for a platform that is not finished
creates an obligation to deliver. Only offer this with a clear scope and date, and
ideally not until the completion work is closer to done.

---

### 8 · The Society's own reserves or a member levy

Listed for completeness. **Do not lead with it.** Asking a professional body to spend
its reserves on a member's product is the hardest version of this ask and reintroduces
every conflict-of-interest problem the parked loan had.

If the Society offers, that is different — accept gratefully and insist it goes through
full governance.

## How to put the ask in the room

Do not ask them to fund it. Ask them to help you find the route.

> I'm not asking the Society for money, and I'm not asking it to borrow — I looked at a
> bank facility and set it aside.
>
> What I'm asking is this. There are routes to funding this that a professional body can
> open and I can't. A donor will fund the Society for construction-sector capacity
> building; they won't fund me. A sponsor will pay to be associated with your CPD
> programme; they won't pay to be associated with mine. And members already have to buy
> CPD every year.
>
> I've listed the ones I can see. I'd like your view on which are worth pursuing, and
> who I should be talking to.

**Then hand over this page and stop talking.**

The last sentence is the important one. A resolution to "explore funding" is worth very
little; **a name and an introduction is worth a great deal.** Leave with a person to
contact, not a good feeling.

---

## What to do if they say "we'll consider it"

That is the most likely outcome of any first meeting with a committee, and it is not a
failure. Convert it into something checkable before you leave the room:

- Who takes it forward, by name
- When they next meet, and whether this is on that agenda
- What they need from you before then, and by when
- Whether you may approach a specific funder using the Society's name, and who signs
  that off

Write the answers down in the room, and put them in the follow-up email within 24 hours.
