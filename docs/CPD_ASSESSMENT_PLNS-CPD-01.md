**Course:** ISO 19650 in Practice: Delivering a Compliant Information Model
**Provider:** Planscape Ltd · **Version:** 1.0 · **Date:** 2026-08-26

| | |
|---|---|
| **Questions** | 15 (7 multiple choice, 8 short answer / applied) |
| **Total marks** | 40 |
| **Pass mark** | 28 / 40 (70%) |
| **Time allowed** | 10 minutes in-session, or 20 minutes if run as a standalone paper |
| **Materials permitted** | Closed book. The delegate field guide is **not** permitted during assessment. |
| **Resit** | One free resit, within 30 days, on a variant paper |

---

## Design notes for the accreditation panel

**Every question maps to a stated learning outcome.** The mapping table is at §3. No
question tests material not taught, and no outcome is left untested.

**Suitability codes are tested only on their stable core.** S0–S4 are consistent
across national annexes and project protocols; the upper codes (S5/S6/S7) vary in
both number and wording between the UK National Annex, other annexes, and
individual project BEPs. Testing a delegate on a distinction that legitimately
differs between projects would be unfair and indefensible on challenge. The course
teaches the full range and teaches the *rule* — **the project BEP is authoritative
for the upper codes** — and Q11 tests that rule rather than a contested table.

**No question is answerable by product knowledge.** A delegate who has never seen
Planscape can score full marks. This is deliberate and load-bearing for the
accreditation.

**Applied questions outweigh recall.** 24 of 40 marks require the delegate to
produce or judge something, not recognise it. Recall-only assessment lets people
pass without competence.

### Section A — Multiple choice (14 marks)

| Q | Answer | Marks | Tests | Notes for the marker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | **B** — Appointing party | 2 | LO1 | The client who commissions and receives the information is the appointing party. **Common error: A.** Delegates conflate "the one in charge" with "lead appointed party" — that is the lead *consultant*, not the client. |
| **Q2** | **C** — OIR → AIR → PIR → EIR | 2 | LO1 | Organisational, then asset, then project, then the specific exchange. **Common error: A** (reciting it backwards). Accept only C. |
| **Q3** | **B** — Appointing party issues, lead appointed party responds | 2 | LO1 | The EIR is a client-side demand; the BEP is the supply-side response. **Do not accept D** — "information manager" is a function, not a party, and may sit on either side. |
| **Q4** | **C** — File size | 2 | LO2 | The seven fields are project, originator, volume/system, level, type, role, number. |
| **Q5** | **B** — S1 | 2 | LO3 | Issued *for coordination* → S1. **Common error: C.** S3 is for review and comment — a different purpose, and the distinction matters contractually. |
| **Q6** | **C** — Only the authorised approver | 2 | LO3 | The defining property of Published is controlled write access under an approval process. **A** is WIP behaviour; **B/D** describe a shared drive. |
| **Q7** | **B** — Pre-appointment BEP | 2 | LO4 | Submitted with the tender, before contract. |

### Section B — Short answer (26 marks)

---

**Q8 — Seven fields in order.** *(4 marks)*

> **Project — Originator — Volume/System — Level/Location — Type — Role — Number**

**Marking:** ½ mark per correct field named (3½ available), ½ mark for correct
sequence. Award the sequence mark only if all seven are present and correctly
ordered.

**Accept:** "Volume" or "System" or "Volume/System" for field 3. "Level" or
"Location" for field 4. Minor wording variation is fine — the delegate must
demonstrate they know what the field *does*, not recite a label.

**Do not accept:** suitability or revision listed as a field. These are **metadata
attributes**, not part of the required name. This is a frequent and important
error — flag it verbally when returning papers.

---

**Q9 — Construct a container name.** *(4 marks)*

> **KIH-PLNS-ZZ-02-DR-M-0017**

**Marking:**

| Element | Mark | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `KIH` project code | ½ | Given in the question |
| `PLNS` originator | ½ | Given in the question |
| `ZZ` volume/system | 1 | **The discriminating mark.** ZZ = whole project / not subdivided. A delegate who invents a zone code has not understood the field. |
| `02` level | ½ | Accept `02`. Do not accept `L02` or `2`. |
| `DR` type | 1 | Drawing. **Common error: `M3`** — that is a 3D model. The question says *drawing*. |
| `M` role | ½ | Mechanical |
| `0017` number | ½ | Must be zero-padded to four digits. `17` scores zero for this element. |

**Cap at 4.** Award a stated assumption (e.g. *"assuming four-digit numbering per
the project BEP"*) as evidence of understanding, but it earns no separate mark —
it may rescue a delegate who used a defensible alternative convention and said so.

**Full credit for a different but internally consistent convention only if the
delegate explicitly states the convention they are working to.** Real projects vary;
a delegate who says so is demonstrating exactly the judgement the course teaches.

---

**Q10 — S1 versus S2.** *(3 marks)*

> **S1 — Suitable for coordination.** The information is issued so other task teams
> can position and design their own work against it. It is expected to change.
>
> **S2 — Suitable for information.** The information is issued so others are aware
> of it. It is *not* an invitation to coordinate against, and carries no expectation
> that the recipient will design to it.

**Marking:** 1 mark per correct definition (2), 1 mark for a valid example pair.

**Valid examples:** S1 — a structural frame model issued so MEP can route services
through it. S2 — a site survey, a planning constraint drawing, or a specification
issued for the team's awareness.

**The distinction that earns the marks:** S1 carries an *expectation of use*; S2
does not. A delegate who writes only "S1 is for coordination, S2 is for
information" has restated the label without explaining it — award 1 mark total.

---

**Q11 — Which suitability table is correct.** *(3 marks)*

> **Both may be correct.** Suitability codes above S4 vary between national annexes,
> published guidance and individual project protocols. There is no single universal
> table.
>
> **The rule: the project's BEP is authoritative.** Whatever suitability schedule the
> BEP defines is the one that governs that project, and it must be agreed before
> information exchange begins.

**Marking:** 1 mark for recognising that variation is legitimate rather than one
source being wrong; 2 marks for stating the BEP-is-authoritative rule.

**Award full marks** to a delegate who answers only *"check the BEP — it defines the
codes for the project"*. That is the operationally correct answer and is the entire
point of the question.

**Award zero** for picking one table and declaring the other wrong, however
confidently argued.

---

**Q12 — Why a shared folder is not a CDE.** *(4 marks)*

Any **three** of the following, 1⅓ marks each, rounded to the delegate's benefit:

- **No state transitions.** There is no WIP → Shared → Published mechanism, so the
  status of any file is a matter of opinion.
- **No access control tied to state.** Anyone can overwrite anything at any point,
  including approved information.
- **No audit trail.** You cannot answer *who approved this, and when* — the question
  that matters when a submission is challenged.
- **No suitability or revision metadata** attached to and travelling with the
  container.
- **No single source of truth.** Multiple versions of the same information coexist
  with nothing designating which is authoritative.

**Marking:** the answer must identify *mechanisms*, not sentiment. "It's not secure
enough" or "it's disorganised" earns nothing. "You can't tell who approved a file"
earns full credit for that point.

Naming files correctly is a **necessary but not sufficient** condition — a delegate
who explicitly makes that observation may be awarded the fourth mark.

---

**Q13 — Revision code.** *(3 marks)*

> **C01.** The **C** signifies a construction-stage issue — the information is now
> issued for construction rather than as a pre-construction (preliminary) revision.

**Marking:** 2 marks for `C01`, 1 mark for correctly explaining the letter.

**Do not accept `P04`** — the point of the question is that crossing from
pre-construction into construction **restarts the numbering under a new letter**,
it does not continue the P sequence.

**Accept** a delegate who notes that the exact scheme is defined in the project BEP,
provided they still give C01 as the answer under the taught convention.

---

**Q14 — TIDP and MIDP.** *(4 marks)*

> A **Task Information Delivery Plan (TIDP)** is produced by **each task team** and
> lists that team's own deliverables, responsibilities and dates.
>
> The **Master Information Delivery Plan (MIDP)** is compiled by the **lead appointed
> party** by aggregating all the TIDPs into a single federated delivery programme for
> the whole project.

**Marking:** 1 mark — TIDP is per task team. 1 mark — TIDP produced by the task team
itself. 1 mark — MIDP aggregates the TIDPs. 1 mark — MIDP compiled by the lead
appointed party.

**The relationship is the point:** TIDPs are the inputs, the MIDP is the
consolidated output. A delegate who describes both documents accurately but does not
convey that the MIDP is *built from* the TIDPs caps at 2 marks.

---

**Q15 — Minimum MIDP row content.** *(3 marks)*

> 1. **The deliverable** — what information container is to be produced
> 2. **The responsible party** — who owes it
> 3. **The date** — when it is due

**Marking:** 1 mark each.

**Also accept**, in place of any of the three: the **suitability code at delivery**,
or the **milestone / stage** it serves. These are defensible additions from a
delegate thinking about the document as a control instrument.

**Do not accept:** file format, file size, or software version — these are useful
metadata but a MIDP row without *what / who / when* cannot control delivery at all.

## §3 — Outcome coverage map

| Learning outcome | Questions | Marks | % of paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LO1** — Parties, roles, requirement chain | Q1, Q2, Q3 | 6 | 15% |
| **LO2** — Construct and explain container names | Q4, Q8, Q9 | 10 | 25% |
| **LO3** — CDE states, suitability, revision | Q5, Q6, Q10, Q11, Q12, Q13 | 17 | 42.5% |
| **LO4** — BEP drafting and types | Q7 | 2 | 5% |
| **LO5** — MIDP construction | Q14, Q15 | 5 | 12.5% |
| | **Total** | **40** | **100%** |

**LO6** (evaluate a live project) is assessed by **Exercise 4**, the private
self-audit, and is deliberately not examined — a delegate cannot be marked on the
compliance of a project the examiner has not seen. Note this explicitly in the
accreditation submission so the panel does not read it as an omission.

**On the weighting:** LO3 carries 42.5% because the CDE and its state machine are
where practice most often fails, and where a misunderstanding causes the most
contractual damage. LO4 is under-weighted in the written paper because BEP drafting
is assessed directly and more meaningfully by **Exercise 3**, which is collected and
marked. Combine the two when reporting competence.

## §4 — Answer sheet and administration

**Layout.** Question paper on one side; a separate single-sheet answer form with
Section A as a circling grid and Section B as ruled boxes sized to the marks
available. Do not let delegates write on the question paper — you will want to reuse
it, and a clean paper is easier to invigilate.

**Delegate identification.** Name, professional registration number, and board.
The registration number is what the certificate is issued against, so capture it
correctly at this point rather than chasing it afterwards.

**Marking turnaround.** Mark same-day where possible. Certificates go out within
five working days, per the syllabus commitment — the marking is not the bottleneck,
certificate production is, so start both together.

**Borderline scripts (26–27 / 40).** Re-mark Section B in full before recording a
fail. Short-answer marking has genuine spread between markers, and a delegate two
marks short deserves the second read.

**Variant paper for resits.** Change the scenarios, not the concepts: a different
project code and discipline in Q9, a different pair of suitability codes in Q10,
a different starting revision in Q13. Keep the outcome map and the marks identical
so the two papers are equivalent in difficulty.

**Retain scripts for two years.** A CPD board may audit a provider's assessment
records. A provider who cannot produce marked scripts on request risks
accreditation.

## §5 — What to do with the results

The marks are for the board. **The pattern is for you.**

Track which questions the room gets wrong. A cohort that collectively fails Q12 has
told you that the CDE concept did not land, and Module 3 needs rewriting before the
next seminar. A cohort that fails Q9 on the `DR`/`M3` type code has told you that
Module 2 moved too fast.

This is the only feedback loop you get on teaching quality, and it is a good one.
Review the item analysis after every cohort and adjust the module, not the question.
