### A2. Statement of need

ISO 19650-1 and -2 are increasingly cited in tender documents by donor-funded
programmes, international consultants and, progressively, national procuring
entities. Regional practitioners are being asked to produce BIM Execution Plans,
Master Information Delivery Plans and structured Common Data Environments —
frequently without ever having been trained in the standard that defines them.

The observable failure pattern is not incompetence. It is that firms adopt the
*vocabulary* of ISO 19650 without the *mechanics*: a folder named "Shared" that
has no state transition rules, files named to a convention that nobody enforces
after week three, and a BEP written once and never revisited. The information
produced does not survive audit, and the practice carries contractual exposure it
cannot see.

This course closes the gap between knowing the standard exists and being able to
run a project to it on Monday morning.

### A3. Learning outcomes

On completion, a delegate will be able to:

1. **Identify** the parties, roles and information requirement chain defined in
   ISO 19650-1 (OIR → AIR → PIR → EIR) and state which party owes which
   information to whom.
2. **Construct** a compliant information container name from a project brief,
   correctly populating all seven fields, and **explain** the function of each field.
3. **Route** an information container through the four CDE states
   (WIP → Shared → Published → Archived) and **assign** the correct suitability
   code (S0–S4) or authorization code (A/B, on published information) and revision
   code (P/C) at each transition.
4. **Draft** the core clauses of a BIM Execution Plan, and **distinguish** a
   pre-appointment from a post-appointment BEP.
5. **Build** a Master Information Delivery Plan from a delivery programme,
   identifying deliverables, responsible parties and target dates.
6. **Evaluate** an existing project's information management against the standard
   and **name** at least three specific non-conformances with their remedies.

*Outcomes are written as observable actions so the assessment can test them
directly. Outcome 6 is the one that changes behaviour — delegates audit their own
live project during the session.*

### A5. Assessment

**Written** — 15 multiple-choice and short-answer questions mapped to outcomes
1–5. Sample items:

- *A drawing is issued for the design team to coordinate against. Which suitability
  code applies, and which CDE container does it sit in?*
- *In the chain OIR → AIR → PIR → EIR, which party issues the EIR and which
  responds to it?*
- *Name the seven fields of an information container name in order.*

**Practical** — Exercises 1–3 are collected and marked. Exercise 4 is private to
the delegate and is not collected; it exists to make the standard personal.

**Pass mark** 70%. A delegate below the mark may resit once at no charge.

### A8. Vendor neutrality — read this before submitting

**A CPD board will reject a course that reads as a product demonstration, and it
should.** The integrity of the accreditation is the entire reason the channel
works. Three rules that must hold in the submission and in the room:

1. **Modules 1–4 are taught standard-first and are tool-agnostic.** The naming
   convention, the state machine, the suitability codes and the BEP structure are
   ISO 19650, not Planscape. A delegate who leaves and buys nothing must still
   have received full value. Exercises 1–3 are done on paper and in a spreadsheet
   for exactly this reason.

2. **Software appears only as the instructor's working method.** In Module 5,
   demonstrating how compliance is verified at scale necessarily requires a tool —
   and the instructor uses the one they use on live projects. That is honest and
   permissible. Naming alternatives where they exist (ACC, Revizto, BIMcollab)
   strengthens rather than weakens the course.

3. **The commercial offer sits outside the accredited hours.** The four contact
   hours end at 13:00. An **optional 30-minute clinic from 13:15** — announced as
   optional, attendance not required for points — is where software is installed
   and configured for those who want it. Keeping this boundary clean protects the
   accreditation and, in practice, the people who stay are self-selected and
   convert far better than a captive room.

## Part B — Delivery notes

*Not submitted to the board. This is how the day is actually run.*

### B1. The three case failures that open the day

Do not open with the standard. Open with cost. Twenty minutes of concrete regional
failure earns you the attention to teach the abstract material afterwards.

- **The rejected submission** — a donor-funded package returned for information
  non-conformance; weeks lost, fee at risk.
- **The empty handover** — a completed facility handed over with drawings and no
  structured asset data; the FM team rebuilding it manually.
- **The coordination cycle that never converged** — clashes re-reported every
  month because no one controlled which model version was authoritative.

Anonymise properly. Use failures you have personally seen. Invented examples are
audible.

### B2. The line that does the work

In Module 3, the sentence that reframes the room:

> *"A shared folder is a place to put files. A CDE is a set of rules about who may
> change a file's status, and a record of every time it happened. If you cannot
> answer 'who approved this, and when', you do not have a CDE — you have a folder
> with an ambitious name."*

Most delegates believe they have a CDE. Most have a folder. That realisation is
the moment the course lands, and it is also the moment the commercial need becomes
obvious without you having to assert it.

### B3. Why Exercise 4 is the important one

Delegates audit their *own live project*, privately, against six criteria. Nobody
collects it. Nobody sees their answers.

This is deliberate. A person who publicly admits their project is non-compliant
gets defensive; a person who discovers it privately gets motivated. They leave
with a specific, personal, unresolved problem — and the knowledge that you solve
it. No pitch required, and none should be made.

### B4. Running the 13:15 clinic

Announce it twice — once at the start, once at 12:55 — as optional and unrelated
to points. Expect 30–50% to stay.

Do not present. Install the free plugin on their machine, open *their* model, and
run the naming and tagging pass on it live. The demonstration is: their own
project, their own mess, fixed in front of them. Ninety seconds against the four
hours they know it would take by hand.

Then stop. Answer questions. Do not close.
